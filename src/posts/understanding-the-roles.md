After [the rise of the forward deployed engineer](#/blog/the-rise-of-the-forward-deployed-engineer), here's the role guide I'd want when reading job descriptions. Compare what people own, what they deliver and how success is judged. Responsibilities can differ considerably between companies using the same title.

## What the work usually centers on

| Role | Main responsibility | What to clarify |
|---|---|---|
| Account executive | Understand the buyer, develop the opportunity and negotiate the agreement. | Revenue targets, account ownership and technical support. |
| [Sales / solutions engineer](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/sales-engineers.htm) | Establish technical fit through discovery, demonstrations, evaluations and troubleshooting. | If the prototype becomes part of the customer's expectations, who approves and maintains it? |
| [Solutions architect](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/10559416/startup-solutions-architect-early-stage-startups) | Advise on architecture, integration, cost and growth; sometimes build prototypes. | Who implements the design, and who owns the result after handoff? |
| [Forward deployed engineer](https://blog.palantir.com/dev-versus-delta-demystifying-engineering-roles-at-palantir-ad44c2a6e87) | Scope a customer problem, build and deploy software, and iterate on the outcome. | What stays custom, what becomes shared product, and who owns failures after launch? |
| Infrastructure FDE | Make deployments work within customer networks, identity systems and operational constraints. | Responsibility for upgrades, observability, recovery and support. |
| [Deployment strategist / forward-deployed PM](https://blog.palantir.com/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-palantir-deployment-strategist-951cb59a5a96) | Define useful workflows, priorities, adoption and measures of success with users. | How much implementation accompanies the product and coordination work? |
| Product engineer / SWE / member of technical staff | Build and maintain product capabilities or shared platform components. | Actual scope: the title alone doesn't establish customer duties. |

These are working distinctions. Palantir's description of deployment strategists includes people who code extensively and people who mostly coordinate. AWS's startup architect role explicitly includes building and choosing an architecture appropriate to the company's current stage. Neither title fixes a person's technical depth or appetite for complexity.

## Technical sales is a technical career

Sales and solutions engineering deserve serious consideration. Understanding a buyer's system, finding an integration constraint and demonstrating whether a product meets the requirement all take technical judgment. The [BLS description](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/sales-engineers.htm) includes requirements analysis, product adaptation and troubleshooting alongside selling.

Some roles conclude with a successful evaluation or a well-supported buying decision. Others extend through implementation. Ask where that boundary sits, especially if your goal is to write and maintain production software. A technically demanding job can have a different output from a software engineering job.

## Start from the part you enjoy

If you enjoy business processes, asking questions and understanding why people work a certain way, investigate technical sales, deployment strategy and FDE. For FDE, build the engineering ability to implement what you discover.

If you enjoy systems, look closely at infrastructure deployment: networking, permissions, failure recovery and the practical consequences of architecture. If you'd rather improve a capability used across many customers, investigate product and platform teams.

As I argued in [getting into AI from another major](#/blog/getting-into-ai-from-another-major), your major can suggest useful strengths. The responsibilities tell you which skills you still need to develop.

## Ask before accepting the title

- What did someone in this role ship last month?
- What would I own after the pilot finishes?
- Who reviews my code, and who responds when it breaks?
- How much time goes to customers, implementation and internal coordination?
- Can I change the product, or only configure it?
- Is success measured by revenue, adoption, reliability, delivery or some combination?

I'd ask for a recent example behind each answer. A real week of work tells you much more than a polished title.
